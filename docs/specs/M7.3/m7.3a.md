# Spec: M7.3a — Attendee sign-in and account

- **Milestone:** M7.3a (Phase 7 plan `docs/plans/mobile.md`, decision P7-5; ADR 0010 and ADR 0025)
- **Status:** Built (2026-10-14). App code in `apps/mobile` and `packages/mobile-core`, plus two small `/v1` additions. Fake providers throughout; no Apple, Google, Stripe, Sentry, EAS or store account was used.
- **Risk tags:** `auth` (new `DELETE /v1/me` and `POST /v1/auth/sign-out-everywhere`), `mobile-contract`
- **Related ADRs/decisions:** 0002 (one command model), 0010 (auth), 0014 (allowlists), 0025 (mobile apps), P7-5, P7-8

## 1. Goal and users
A person who opens the Yayatoh app for the first time can sign in by any of the ways the server offers (emailed code, Sign in with Apple, Google, a password, with the two-factor and email-proof steps), land on the tab shell, change their language and appearance, sign out here or everywhere, and delete their account (App Review 5.1.1(v)).

## 2. What was built

### Server (`auth` change: say so when approving)
- **`DELETE /v1/me`** (`deleteMe`): deletes the signed-in person's account with the web's own `deleteAccount` (`packages/modules/privacy/src/account.ts`), as `by: self`. It needs a step-up within the last ten minutes (`POST /v1/auth/step-up`; a fresh sign-in counts): without one the answer is `401 step_up_required` and nothing is deleted. Refused with `409 invalid_state` (`details.reason: last_owner`, `details.orgs`) while the person is the only owner of an organization. On success it answers `200 {requestId, summary}` (counts only); the confirmation mail goes to the old address first (`V1Deps.accountNotice`, default: the live provider or dev mailbox, else a redacted log line, as the web), the account is anonymised, its sessions and refresh-token families end, the address joins the suppression list, and each org detaches its link (orders and tickets stay with the organizer). A password account that never verified its email can delete too. Both routes refuse a session a staff member is acting through (`403 forbidden`, `details.reason: impersonation`), as the web's `ownSession` rule does: `BearerSession.impersonated` is read from the session's `impersonation_id`.
- **`POST /v1/auth/sign-out-everywhere`** (`signOutEverywhere`): ends every session of the person (web, tenant hosts, API clients) and revokes every refresh-token family, so no phone can refresh its way back in. `AppSignIn.signOutEverywhere` calls the web's `TwoFactorService.signOutEverywhere` and then `revokeAllRefreshTokens(userId, at, 'signed_out')` (a new optional reason argument; the existing check constraint already allows it). Audited as `app_sign_in.signed_out_everywhere`.
- `/v1` changes are additions only. `apps/api/openapi.json`, `packages/sdk/src/schema.ts` and the automation apps are regenerated; `pnpm contracts:check` is clean. `api-v1` now depends on `@yayatoh/privacy`.
- **Not changed:** the web's "sign out everywhere" (`signOutEverywhereAction`) still ends sessions only; it does not revoke refresh-token families. An app refresh token could mint a new session after it. Left for the owner to decide (see the owner inbox); the new route is the one the app uses.
- No migration, no new table, no hand-written SQL.

### `packages/mobile-core`
- **`core.account`** (`session/account.ts`): `signOutEverywhere()` (server first, then this phone signs out and wipes; if the server cannot be reached nothing changes and the outcome is `unavailable`) and `deleteAccount()` (`deleted` after signing out and wiping, `stepUpRequired`, `lastOwner` with the organization names, `rateLimited`, `unavailable`, `failed`). Both return plain outcomes and never throw for something that went the way the server meant.

### `apps/mobile`
- **Routes (all mine):** `index.tsx` (reads the stored session; signed-in people go to Tickets, others to the welcome screen; a loading state in between), `(auth)/` (`_layout`, `welcome`, `email`, `code`, `password`, `two-factor`, `link-proof`), `(tabs)/_layout.tsx` and `(tabs)/account.tsx`, `account/` (`_layout`, `language`, `delete`, `deleted`). Placeholders `(tabs)/tickets.tsx` and `(tabs)/discover.tsx` only until M7.3b and M7.3c merge; the merge keeps theirs. `account/notifications.tsx` is M7.3d's; the Account tab links to it.
- **Welcome:** what the app does, then "Continue with email", "Sign in with Apple" (Apple's own button, iOS only), "Continue with Google" (only when the build has a Google OAuth client id for the platform) and "Use a password". With no network on first launch it says so and offers "Try again" (it also recovers by itself when the phone reconnects).
- **Email code:** address (validated), then six digits: a number pad, the one-time-code hint so iOS and Android offer the code, paste cleaned to digits, signs in on the sixth digit. Wrong and expired codes are told apart in words only as far as the server tells them apart (it answers `invalid_code` for both, so the message says "wrong or has expired"). Resend is held for 30 seconds after a code goes out, and for as long as the server asks when it answers 429 (`Retry-After`), with the wait in minutes. Too many wrong codes or a spent challenge offer "Start again".
- **Password, Apple, Google:** through `core.signIn`. The two-factor step takes an authenticator or backup code in one field; the email-proof step takes the code emailed to the existing account. A step from any first factor (including the email-proof step) leads to the next screen. A closed Apple or Google sheet is not an error; an unavailable sheet and every server refusal have their own messages.
- **Tabs:** Tickets, Discover and Account in `(tabs)/_layout.tsx`, signed-in people only; a signed-out visitor to any tab goes to the welcome screen, a signed-in person to `/welcome` goes to Tickets.
- **Account:** name, email and "Verified" (read-only), language (a picker of the phone's language and the 13 the app is translated into), appearance (system, light, dark), a link to notification settings, sign out (asks first), sign out everywhere (asks first, tells the person if the server could not be reached), and delete account.
- **Deletion:** says what is deleted and what organizers keep, then asks the person to confirm it is them (the server's step-up method: authenticator or backup code, password, or an emailed code) and to type their address again; deletes through `core.account.deleteAccount()`; the phone is signed out and wiped; the "account deleted" page is shown to the signed-out phone. A person who is the only owner of an organization is told which ones, before they have lost anything.
- **State between screens** (the address, the single-use challenge) lives in memory only, never in the route.
- **Strings:** namespaces `welcome`, `signIn`, `account` in `apps/mobile/messages/*.json` in all 13 locales (101 keys each; ICU plurals for minutes, seconds and the organization count; Arabic zero/one/two/few/many/other, Russian one/few/many/other, Japanese and Chinese other). The shared sign-in failures, rate limit, language and theme strings come from `core`.
- **Shared files touched, additively:** `apps/mobile/jest.config.js` (`standard-navigation` is transformed, Expo Router's test renderer needs it), `packages/mobile-core/src/core.ts` and `index.ts` (`core.account`). The root `_layout.tsx` and `src/core.ts` of the app are untouched. `home.*` strings remain in the catalogs (dead since the placeholder home screen was replaced); another builder may still rely on them.

## 3. Not yet / Later
- **Edit name** and **change email**: `/v1` has no route to change either, so the Account tab shows them read-only.
- **Forgot password** in the app: there is no `/v1` route; the password screen points to the email code instead.
- **Passkeys** are Later (P7-5).
- A native Google account picker (the system-browser session is used, see the owner inbox from M7.1c).
- The Apple and Google buttons carry text only; Google's brand mark needs the owner's asset decision.
- Tab icons: the bar has labels only for now.
- Device pass: Sign in with Apple and Google on a real phone; autofill of the emailed code; the three Maestro flows below; large-text and screen-reader walkthroughs; the native layout at the largest system font size.
