## M3.10b — refund operations

**Acceptance (roadmap):** cancelling an event with 1,000 orders refunds in a resumable batch, skips disputed charges and reconciles.

Everything runs on the fake payment provider (live Stripe waits for the owner's account). Defaults the owner has not decided are marked **pending owner** and listed in `docs/owner-inbox.md`.

### Unified order timeline
- `reports.orderTimeline` (`orders:read`; reports is tier 5, so it may read orders, ticketing, check-in and notifications): purchase, payment (and how it was charged), every ticket issued and voided (with why), claim-link transfers (offered, claimed, withdrawn), door scans (admitted, or refused with the result), messages sent (kind and delivery status), refund requests (asked, approved, declined with the reason), refunds (started, completed or failed, with the reason), and internal notes — oldest first. Allowlisted `OrderTimelineItemDto` (`at, kind, ticketSerial, amountMinor, code, text, who`): only these fields leave.
- Disputes and their evidence are finance data: `disputeTimelineItems` adds opened / evidence submitted / won / lost for members with `finance:read` (the order page already loads disputes only for them).
- The order page shows it in the **event's timezone** (named above the list), with an "Add a note for your team" form.
- **Notes:** `orders.order_notes` (tenant, RLS), `orders.addOrderNote` with the new permission `orders:note` (owners, admins, managers, finance, box office; event managers for their event). Notes are internal (canary class `internal`).

### Refund-request queue
- **Buyers ask** from their order page (manage link; no account): "Refund" section with their own live tickets (all ticked), an optional message, "Ask for a refund". `orders.requestRefund` (`public:refund_request`; the org comes from the manage token, never the request; rate limited per token and client with the new `refundRequest` policy) checks: paid online, something of theirs left to refund, **no other open request** (partial unique index), and the refund policy the order was bought under (a cancelled event always allows: platform minimum). Refusals carry the reason (`policy_window_closed` with the deadline, `policy_no_refunds`, `request_open`). The page then shows where the request stands (open, approved, or declined with the organizer's reason).
- **SLA:** `due_at` = 5 business days (UTC weekdays) after asking — **pending owner**. `order.refund_requested@1` alerts owners, admins and finance (in-app + email, `orders.refund-requested`).
- **The queue** (Console → Refund requests, `/o/{org}/refund-requests`; anyone with `orders:read`): open requests oldest-due first with age ("Waiting 2 days") and "Answer by" with On time / Overdue, plus a summary count; Approved and Declined tabs newest first. Each opens the order.
- **Approve** on the order page (`orders:refund`: owners, admins, finance): "Refund the tickets they asked for" or "Refund an amount instead". It **is** the refund (`orders.startRefund` with `refundRequestId`, through the shared refund flow): same policy engine, large refunds need step-up, the provider call is keyed by the refund id. The request is approved in the same transaction; if the provider declines the refund the request reopens. A request asked in time is answered **under the policy as it stood when the buyer asked** (so a window that closed while it waited does not block it). A full refund of the order by any route answers an open request. The buyer is emailed by the usual refund email.
- **Decline** with a reason (3–500 characters, `orders.declineRefundRequest`): the buyer sees it on the order page and is emailed it (`orders.refund-declined`). Answered requests stay visible on the order with their outcome.

### Refund policy editor: tighten only for future orders
- The policy shown at purchase is **snapshotted on the order** (`orders.refund_policy_snapshot`: kind, days before, kept per ticket; `unset` when none was shown). The text shown to the buyer is rendered from it (per locale, deadline in the event's timezone).
- A later policy change is compared with each order's snapshot (`isTighter`): refunds closing earlier or never, or more kept per ticket, is tightening. For every refund of that order both policies are evaluated and the buyer gets the better answer (`evaluateOrderRefundPolicy`) — so **a tightening only reaches orders placed after it**, a loosening reaches everyone. Orders bought with no policy shown, or before M3.10b, follow the current policy (nothing was promised). Both the buyer's order page and the organizer's order page show the policy the order is under.
- `orders.setRefundPolicy` returns `tightened` and `ordersKeepingTerms` (additive); the editor says "Stricter policy saved. It applies to orders placed from now on; N orders already sold keep the terms they were bought under." and always explains the rule under the form. The audit row records `tightened`.

### Cancel / postpone wizard
- **Entry:** "Cancel or postpone" on the event home (event editors, published/postponed/cancelled events) → `/o/{org}/e/{event}/cancel`. Viewers and anyone without `events:write` get a 404.
- **Choose:** cancel (and refund everyone) or postpone (keyboard-friendly radio group).
- **Financial preview** (`orders.cancellationPreview`, `orders:refund`, pure math in `domain/mass-refund.ts`): paid orders, gross, booking fees, already refunded; per funds flow (charged by Yayatoh for the organizer / on the organizer's payout account) the orders, what is refunded, the fees returned and the organizer's share; what is left out — **open chargebacks** (no buyer is paid twice) and **money the organizer collected** (box office: refunded in person). Nothing moves until the organizer ticks the confirmation.
- **Cancel and refund:** the action checks step-up first (so the event is never cancelled by a submission that cannot start the refunds), cancels the event (`events:write`) and starts the mass refund (`orders.startMassRefund`: `orders:refund`, **step-up**, category `money`: refused while staff act as a member). On an event already cancelled it only offers "Refund every buyer".
- **Postpone:** the event goes to `postponed`; `orders.postponement-mailer` emails every buyer who still holds a ticket (`events.postponed`: tickets stay valid; refunds by request under the policy; over 90 days without a new date the platform minimum applies). The header "Postpone" button sends the same notice.

### Resumable mass refund
- **Tables:** `orders.mass_refunds` (one live run per event: partial unique index; status running / paused / done; counters and the refunded total, with a CHECK that they add up; the reconciliation) and `orders.mass_refund_items` (one per order, snapshotted at start in the orders' creation order; status pending / refunded / skipped_disputed / skipped / failed; the item's refund; a stable code).
- **The batch** (`runMassRefund`, system actor only through `platform:orders.mass_refund`): `orders.nextMassRefundStep` takes the next pending item under row locks and either settles it without the provider (open dispute → `skipped_disputed`; organizer-collected → `skipped`; nothing left → `skipped`) or records its refund — **in the same transaction as the item's `refund_id`** — and returns the provider instructions. The provider is asked outside any transaction, keyed `refund:<refundId>`; `orders.completeRefund` records the answer (tickets void, ledger, receivable/transfer reversal after payout, `order.refunded@1` → the buyer's refund email) and `orders.settleMassRefundItem` counts the item once. **Idempotent per order:** a crash between the provider and the database leaves the item's refund pending, and the next step hands back the *same* refund, so the provider (and Stripe's idempotency) never refunds twice; a settled item never changes.
- **Refund amounts:** the platform minimum — every live ticket at its all-in price, fee back. If earlier partial refunds leave less than that, the rest goes back as an amount with the fee not yet returned, and the live tickets are voided.
- **Pause / resume** (`orders.pauseMassRefund` / `orders.resumeMassRefund`, `orders:refund`, audited): a paused run is not worked (the order in flight completes). **Progress** on the wizard page (auto-refresh while running): n of total, refunded, open chargeback, left out, failed, the refunded amount, and the orders left out with why.
- **Reconciliation at the end:** the platform cash the ledger moved for the run's refunds must equal what they gave back (platform charges: the whole amount; direct charges: the platform's fee part), one refund journal per refund, the item amounts must add up to the run's total, and every item processed; the result and the organizer's resulting debt (receivables from the refunds, less transfer reversals) are stored on the run and shown ("Reconciled: the books moved exactly what was refunded").
- **pg-boss job** `orders.mass-refund` (tenant job, `{orgId, runId}`) on an **exclusive** queue keyed by the run: at most one job per run queued or active. The worker leader queues every running run every 3 s (`enqueueDueMassRefunds`, platform_reader, audited); each job works ~25 s and ends, so pause/resume and redeploys are picked up between orders. Dev and CI (no worker) use `POST /api/dev/mass-refunds/run` (`org`, optional `run`, `limit`; 404 unless dev auth).

### Data, migration and privacy
Migration `0060_lovely_slipstream.sql` (renumbered at merge): new tables `orders.refund_requests`, `orders.order_notes`, `orders.mass_refunds`, `orders.mass_refund_items` (all `tenantTable`: org_id, ENABLE + FORCE RLS, canonical policy, org-leading indexes, composite FKs, org-scoped uniques), and the nullable column `orders.orders.refund_policy_snapshot` (metadata-only). Hand-written (between the markers): composite FKs `refund_requests_event_fk` and `mass_refunds_event_fk` to `events.events` (down the tiers; new tables, so no `NOT VALID` needed). Nothing destructive. Fixture rows for both orgs (a declined and an open request, a note, a finished run with a skipped item); private columns declared (`message` personal, `decline_reason` holder on declined rows, notes internal).
